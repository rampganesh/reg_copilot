# Validating the Right Source

## Statement

Finding the first potentially relevant document is easy, but establishing that it is the **right source**, that it is applicable to the question being asked, and that it has **not been superseded** by a later publication is the hardest part of the research task. **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L11)**

## Who has it

- **Analyst 1**: explicitly states that validation is harder than discovery (L9, L11)
- **Analyst 2**: encounters situations where template instructions alone are insufficient and cross-referencing is required (L14–L16)
- **Regulatory Change/Reporting SME**: emphasizes that cross-referencing is "particularly important during a regulatory transition" (L17)
- **Experienced analysts vs. newer analysts**: experienced analysts remember where information is likely to be found, while newer analysts "spend considerably longer searching for the same answer" (L23)

## Evidence

- **The discovery/validation gap**: "finding the first potentially relevant document is generally not the hardest part. The difficulty is establishing that the document is actually the **right source**, that it is applicable to the question being asked, and that it has not been superseded by a later publication." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L11)**
- **Cross-referencing as the norm**: "the same concept can appear in a rule, supervisory statement, reporting instruction, template instruction, policy statement or another related document, but the wording and level of detail can differ. Analysts therefore often follow references from one document to another before they are comfortable answering a question." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L13)**
- **Example requiring cross-reference**: "The analyst had to look at the relevant reporting rule, then check related definitions and previous regulatory material before confirming what the field represented." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L15)**
- **Transition complexity**: "A document may describe the existing requirement while another document describes a future requirement. Analysts need to know whether they are answering a question about the current reporting regime, the future Basel 3.1 regime, or the transition between the two." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L17)**
- **Effective dates are critical**: "An answer that is technically correct but refers to a requirement that is not yet applicable can still be operationally wrong." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L19)**
- **Previous interpretations should not be authoritative**: "An analyst may use a previous interpretation as a starting point, but the underlying regulatory source needs to be checked before the interpretation is reused." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L29)**

## Contradicting evidence

None identified from this source. The difficulty is described as operational reality, not opinion.
