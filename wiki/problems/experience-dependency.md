# Experience dependency and knowledge fragmentation

## Statement
Useful regulatory knowledge is fragmented across personal collections of documents, links, spreadsheets and previous interpretations, and much of it lives in analysts' heads. When experienced analysts are unavailable, others repeat research that has already been done elsewhere, and newer analysts spend considerably longer finding the same answers. There is no single place containing everything needed to answer regulatory questions.

## Who has it
Whole team; biggest cost borne by newer analysts and by anyone covering for an unavailable experienced colleague.

## Evidence
- "Analysts currently maintain their own collections of useful documents, links, spreadsheets and previous interpretations. There is no single place that contains every piece of information required to answer regulatory questions." — Regulatory Reporting Manager (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L21)
- Experienced analysts remember where information is likely to be found; newer analysts can spend considerably longer — Analyst 1 (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L23)
- Analysts ask colleagues through informal channels; the team relies on people who worked on similar changes before — Analyst 2 (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L25)
- "This creates a dependency on individual experience. When an experienced analyst is unavailable, other team members may repeat research that has already been performed elsewhere." — Regulatory Reporting Manager (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L27)

### From Meeting 1 (2025-05-24) — two divergent value propositions
- For experienced analysts, "the biggest potential benefit… may therefore be reducing repetitive searching and evidence collection rather than eliminating expert judgement" — Analyst 1 (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L77)
- But the SME cautioned these are two related, non-identical use cases: "A system designed to answer questions for experienced analysts may need to behave differently from one designed to teach newer analysts how to investigate a regulatory issue." (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L81)
- Implication: the product has at least two value propositions to serve — speed for experienced analysts, and investigation/ramp-up support for newer ones — and a single design may not serve both identically.

### From Meeting 2 (2025-05-26)
- Historical internal answers can be evidence of how the organisation previously interpreted a question, but must not automatically be treated as regulatory authority — Prudential Regulatory SME (source: 2025_05_26_meeting_followup.md, L79)
- Internal answers should be labelled separately from external regulatory sources, especially where they represent judgement rather than regulator statements (source: 2025_05_26_meeting_followup.md, L81)
- The distinction matters if users copy AI-generated answers into formal reporting documentation — Compliance representative (source: 2025_05_26_meeting_followup.md, L83)

### From email digest (2025-06-04)
- Use of internal historical answers as an authoritative knowledge source explicitly listed as an attractive extension, not V1 (source: 2025_06_04_digest_clarifications.md, row 1)

### From interviews (2025-06-17/18) — divergence behaviourally confirmed
- Both analysts teach newer colleagues the research path (where to start, which sources are authoritative, how to spot superseded material, how to validate evidence) rather than giving answers (source: 2025_06_17_interview_analyst1exp.md, L101-109; source: 2025_06_18_interview_analyst2prov.md, L104-110)
- "This is different from how they personally work. Experience means they can often jump directly to likely sources or recognise relevant terminology without consciously following every step" — Analyst 1 (source: 2025_06_17_interview_analyst1exp.md, L111)
- "Newer colleagues often need help understanding **why one source should be trusted over another**, whereas an experienced analyst may recognise this from experience" — Analyst 2 (source: 2025_06_18_interview_analyst2prov.md, L112)
- As newer analysts, both relied heavily on colleagues, previous examples and knowing which documents contained what (source: 2025_06_17_interview_analyst1exp.md, L113; source: 2025_06_18_interview_analyst2prov.md, L114) → personas trigger fired, see wiki/personas/
