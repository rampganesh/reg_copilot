# Knowledge Locked in Individuals

## Statement

"There is no single place that contains every piece of information required to answer regulatory questions." Experienced analysts have an advantage because "they remember where particular information is likely to be found," while newer analysts "spend considerably longer searching for the same answer." The team "frequently relies on people who have worked on similar regulatory changes before." This creates a dependency on individual experience. **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L21, L23, L25, L27)**

## Who has it

- **Regulatory Reporting Manager**: notes the lack of centralized information (L21)
- **Analyst 1**: points to experienced analysts' knowledge advantage (L23)
- **Analyst 2**: describes reliance on colleagues' prior work (L25)
- **SME**: confirms the dependency problem (L27)

## Evidence

- **No centralized knowledge**: "The team currently maintains their own collections of useful documents, links, spreadsheets and previous interpretations. There is no single place that contains every piece of information required to answer regulatory questions." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L21)**
- **Experienced vs. newer analysts**: "experienced analysts have an advantage because they remember where particular information is likely to be found. Newer analysts can spend considerably longer searching for the same answer." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L23)**
- **Reliance on colleagues**: "analysts also ask colleagues for help through informal channels when they cannot find an answer. The team frequently relies on people who have worked on similar regulatory changes before." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L25)**
- **Dependency on individual experience**: "this creates a dependency on individual experience. When an experienced analyst is unavailable, other team members may repeat research that has already been performed elsewhere." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L27)**
- **Newer-analyst ramp**: "Analyst 2 said that the biggest benefit for less experienced analysts may be helping them understand where to look and giving them a starting point for their research." **(source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L79)**

## Contradicting evidence

- **Experienced analysts may prioritize different benefits**: L77 states the biggest potential benefit for experienced analysts is "reducing repetitive searching and evidence collection rather than eliminating expert judgement," suggesting the knowledge-locked problem matters more to newer analysts.
