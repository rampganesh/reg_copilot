# Regulatory Reporting Manager

Owns the initiative. Team prepares and validates UK regulatory capital reporting; preparing for Basel 3.1 transition (1 January 2027) (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L5).

## Positions
- **Core problem framing**: time and effort to find, connect, validate and present regulatory information, not document access (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L99)
- **Success = faster evidence assembly with maintained/improved quality**; adoption depends on experienced analysts believing it saves time (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L73, L75)
- **Narrow initial scope**: a relatively narrow regulatory domain, not every regulatory question (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L83, L109)
- **Guardrails**: system must not submit returns, change production reporting systems, or make decisions entering the reporting process without human review (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L95); escalation is a valid outcome, not product failure (source: L91)
- **Distinguish core vs. extension capabilities** in next-stage discovery (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L107)

## Evidence
- Knowledge fragmentation: analysts maintain personal collections; no single place contains everything (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L21); experience dependency creates repeated research (L27)
- Precise provenance: "a link to a 300-page policy document would not be enough" (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L47)
- Consistency for recurring questions wanted, but not dependency on AI answers without review (source: 2025_05_24_meeting_kickoff.md, L67)
- Endorsed balanced scorecard and deferred capabilities via consolidated digest (source: 2025_06_04_digest_clarifications.md, rows 2-3)
