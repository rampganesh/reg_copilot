# Email —  Answers to open questions

Read below as table

| #     | Survey topic                                                                                        | Consolidated stakeholder response                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Product implication / decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Which capabilities solve the core research problem vs. attractive extensions?**                   | **Core:** authoritative regulatory search; natural-language querying; discovery of related/cross-referenced material; source/version/status awareness; evidence-backed explanations; precise citations; clear indication of uncertainty or insufficient evidence. **Attractive extensions:** sophisticated “What changed?” analysis; downstream impact analysis; autonomous applicability determination; automated reporting/system changes; use of internal historical answers as an authoritative knowledge source.                                                                                                              | V1 should focus on **finding, connecting, and explaining authoritative evidence**. Q&A is a core interaction, but the product should remain evidence-led rather than becoming an autonomous compliance decision-maker.                                                                                   |
| **2** | **Should “What has changed?” (current vs. future requirement comparison) be in the first release?** | A full interpretive **“What changed?” capability should not be a V1 requirement**. However, users need to distinguish current, future, superseded and proposed material because Basel 3.1 creates a transition period. Basic version/effective-date awareness is therefore foundational.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | **Include basic temporal/version awareness in V1; defer sophisticated change analysis.** V1 should allow users to retrieve and compare relevant current/future sources manually, while a later release can automate meaningful change analysis.                                                          |
| **3** | **What success metrics should define the first version?**                                           | Success should cover **productivity, retrieval quality, answer/evidence quality, safe behaviour, and adoption**. Important measures include time to a supported answer, retrieval relevance/recall, citation correctness and completeness, groundedness, SME usefulness, appropriate refusal/escalation, unsupported-answer rate, repeat usage and adoption. A manual-process baseline should be established first.                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Define a balanced V1 scorecard rather than relying on thumbs-up or usage alone. A key north-star outcome could be **reduction in time required to produce a verified, evidence-backed answer without reducing evidence quality or increasing unsafe answers**.                                           |
| **4** | **What counts as sufficiently precise provenance in practice?**                                     | A citation needs to allow an analyst to **independently verify the claim quickly**. Document-level references alone are insufficient for important answers. Provenance should normally identify the document/source, version or status, relevant section, and where applicable the **paragraph, table, reporting instruction/template reference, or page/location**. The system must not fabricate quotations or references.                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Establish a **minimum provenance standard**: source + document/version/status + precise location. For structured reporting material, provenance may need to identify the specific **table, row/field, instruction or template reference**. Evaluation should test citation correctness and completeness. |
| **5** | **What are the inclusion/exclusion criteria for the deliberately defined first-version corpus?**    | **Include:** authoritative, relevant UK capital/regulatory-reporting material needed for the initial use case, with sufficient metadata and version/status information. This includes relevant PRA Rulebook material, policy/supervisory material, reporting instructions/templates and carefully identified supporting regulatory material. **Exclude:** unrelated regulatory domains, uncontrolled web content, material with unclear provenance/status, private/internal bank material for the initial version, and sources that could be mistaken for operative requirements when they are only explanatory/proposed material. | Create a **bounded, curated corpus** rather than ingesting “all regulatory documents.” A designated Regulatory/Prudential SME or Regulatory Content Owner governs inclusion, exclusion, relationships and meaning; Technology owns ingestion/indexing.                                                   |
| **6** | **Should there be a “current requirement” vs. “future requirement” mode?**                          | **Yes, but it must be explicitly date-based.** “Current” should mean applicable **as of a specified/reference date**, while “future” refers to requirements with a future effective/applicability date. Publication date must not be treated as equivalent to applicability. Users should be able to see the effective date/status and distinguish final requirements from proposals.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Include **temporal awareness and explicit as-of/effective-date behaviour in V1**. Avoid ambiguous labels such as simply “latest.” The system should make the temporal basis visible in both search and answers.                                                                                          |
| **7** | **What interface language and status-labelling conventions should the product use?**                | Language should be **evidence-led and appropriately cautious**. Preferred formulations include “Relevant sources indicate…”, “The cited PRA material states…”, and “The following sources appear relevant…”. Where evidence is insufficient, the system should explicitly say so. “The applicable requirement is…” should be reserved for cases where applicability has been validated/approved. Material should be labelled by status/type, e.g. **final/operative, future effective, superseded/historical, proposed/consultation, explanatory/supporting**.                                                                     | Build **status and authority labelling into the core UX**, not as metadata hidden from users. The assistant should never silently resolve conflicting sources or present a proposal as a requirement. Ambiguous/conflicting evidence should trigger an explicit warning or escalation path.              |
